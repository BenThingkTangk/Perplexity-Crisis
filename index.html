Strategic Diagnosis — Why the "Multi-Cloud" Thesis Is Now Backfiring
🔥
The Philosophical Trap
Perplexity's multi-cloud strategy was predicated on the classical thesis: better performance, lower cost, and geographic compliance through provider redundancy. In theory, sound. In practice, the coordination overhead of three competing hyperscalers — each with proprietary tooling, separate SLA structures, and zero incentive to help competitors' customers — has created more operational debt than any single-provider lock-in would have.
⚡
The Timing Problem
The Azure deal (Jan 29) and CoreWeave deal (March 3) were signed 33 days apart. Each requires months of deep integration work — custom Kubernetes configurations, routing logic, observability pipelines, billing reconciliation, and network tuning. Layering a third hyperscaler onto an already-complex AWS foundation before either new deal was fully integrated is a textbook case of compounding integration debt.
💸
The Billing Black Hole
AWS, Azure, and CoreWeave each maintain isolated billing ledgers with incompatible credit structures. There is no native cross-cloud unified cost visibility — it must be built by Perplexity's engineering team using third-party tools (e.g. Spot.io, Apptio, or custom dashboards). Until that abstraction layer exists, users experience credit disappearance, invoice surprises, and unresolved waiver requests like the ticket shown here.
🧩
The Path Forward
Resolution requires a dedicated multi-cloud abstraction layer: a unified control plane (likely Kubernetes-based), a single-pane billing aggregator, and formalized escalation bridges to each hyperscaler's enterprise support org. Without it, Perplexity's engineering capacity is being consumed by infrastructure firefighting rather than product velocity — the most expensive kind of technical debt.
